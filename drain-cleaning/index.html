Drain Cleaning & Unclogging in Meridian, ID

Drain cleaning means clearing a blockage from the pipe itself, not just the fixture, so water drains at full speed again. In Meridian that is the single most requested plumbing job, and it is often urgent. A kitchen sink full of dishwater or a shower that fills to the ankles does not wait politely for business hours.

Our 24/7 line sends a plumber with the equipment to open the line, find out why it clogged, and tell you whether it is likely to happen again.

What to have ready when you call

  • Which drains are slow and which are fine.
  • Whether the washer or dishwasher was running.
  • If you know where your sewer cleanout is.
  • Any past clogs in the same line.

Is it one drain or the main line?

This is the first question a plumber will ask, because the answer changes the tool and the price. One slow sink usually means a clog in that fixture's branch line. Several drains acting up at once points to the main line that carries everything out to the street.

  • The toilet gurgles or bubbles when the washing machine drains.
  • Water rises in the tub or shower when you flush a toilet.
  • A floor drain or basement fixture has water or sewage coming up.
  • More than one bathroom is slow at the same time.
  • You smell sewage near a floor drain or the outdoor cleanout.

If any of those match, stop running water in the house. Every flush or load of laundry pushes more water toward a line that cannot drain it, and the lowest fixture is where it will come out.

Why drains clog in Meridian homes

Meridian's water comes from city wells, and the 2024 water report puts hardness between 6.70 and 10.16 grains per gallon depending on your zone. That mineral content leaves scale on the inside of drain lines over the years. Scale gives grease, soap, and hair something rough to grab onto.

Kitchen lines clog most often. Cooking grease goes down warm and liquid, then cools and coats the pipe a few feet downstream. Add coffee grounds or rice and the opening shrinks fast. A garbage disposal does not change this. It only grinds the food smaller before it settles.

Bathroom clogs are mostly hair bound together by soap scum. Laundry standpipes clog from lint, especially on older homes without a lint trap on the washer outlet.

Main line clogs have different causes depending on the age of the house. In Old Town Meridian, older cast iron and clay pipe can let tree roots in at the joints. In newer subdivisions with PVC laterals, the usual cause is something flushed that should not be, like wipes labeled flushable, or a low spot in the pipe where waste settles.

Clogs follow house age in Garden City too, where many 1950s and 1960s homes still drain through the original clay or cast iron lateral.

Grease and scale clogs are just as common in Ada County's newest subdivisions off Ten Mile Road as in older homes near Main Street, because the hard water is the same.

How we clear the line, and which tool fits which clog

The right machine depends on the pipe size, the pipe material, and what is causing the blockage. Using a too-small cable on a main line only punches a hole through the clog, which is why some drains clog again a week later.

When a main line clears but the cause is unclear, we recommend running a video camera inspection while the line is open. It adds some time to the visit, and it tells you whether you are looking at a one-time clog or a pipe problem.

When a clog keeps coming back

One clog is usually bad luck. Three clogs in the same line within a year usually means something physical is wrong with the pipe.

The common culprits are roots growing through a joint, a belly where the pipe has settled and holds standing water, or a crack that catches debris. None of those can be fixed with a cable. Clearing the line buys time, but the clog will return.

The honest next step is to look inside the pipe. If the camera shows a broken or separated section, that becomes a sewer line repair conversation, which could be a spot repair or a lining depending on the damage.

What affects the price of drain cleaning

We do not quote a flat number over the phone for every clog, because two calls that sound the same can be very different jobs. These are the factors that move the price.

  • Access. A clean, reachable cleanout is quicker than pulling a toilet or working through a roof vent.
  • Location of the clog. A branch line is less work than a main line 60 feet out.
  • Time of day. After-hours emergency visits cost more than a scheduled daytime appointment.
  • Whether a camera inspection is added to confirm the cause.
  • What the line is made of and whether it needs jetting instead of a cable.

What to expect after the line is cleared

A cleared drain should run fast and quiet. A few checks in the following days tell you whether the fix will last.

  • Run hot water for a few minutes in each fixture that was affected.
  • Listen for gurgling at nearby toilets and tubs, which can mean the main is only partly open.
  • Watch floor drains and the lowest fixture for any slow return of water.
  • If a camera was run, keep the video with your home records.

If the same line slows down again within a month, that is a sign of buildup or pipe damage the cable could not remove, and it is worth asking about jetting or a camera look before the next backup.

Keeping Meridian drains clear between visits

Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing them. Put a strainer in every shower. Run hot water for 30 seconds after using the disposal so food moves all the way down the line.

If you know where your sewer cleanout is, keep it uncovered by bark, gravel, or landscaping. The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), which Idaho's plumbing code builds on, requires cleanouts to stay accessible for exactly this reason. The City of Meridian maintains the sewer in the public right of way, but the lateral on your property is your responsibility, and a clear cleanout makes every future service call faster.

Chemical drain openers are worth skipping. They rarely clear a real blockage, and they leave caustic water sitting in the pipe for the plumber to work around.

Questions before you call

Drain cleaning questions from Meridian homeowners

Can you come out at night for a clogged drain?

Yes. If a drain or main line is backing up, call (208) 679-8357 any time. If it is one slow sink and the rest of the house works, we may suggest a morning visit to save you the after-hours rate.

Why does my toilet gurgle when the washer drains?

Gurgling means air is being pushed through the trap because water cannot move down the main line fast enough. That points to a partial main line clog, and it is worth clearing before it becomes a backup.

Do you clean drains for rentals and property managers?

Yes. We work with landlords and managers around Meridian. Let us know who should approve the work and who gets the invoice when you call.

Where is my sewer cleanout?

Many Meridian homes have a capped pipe near the foundation or in the front yard between the house and the street. Some older homes have it in the crawl space. If you cannot find it, the plumber can locate it.

Should I try a chemical drain cleaner first?

We advise against it. Chemical openers seldom clear a real clog and can damage older pipe. Tell the plumber if you have already used one so they can protect themselves from the residue.

Will cleaning the drain fix the problem for good?

If the clog was grease, hair, or something flushed, usually yes. If it keeps returning, a camera inspection will show whether the pipe itself needs repair.
