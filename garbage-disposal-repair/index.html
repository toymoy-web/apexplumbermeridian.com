Garbage Disposal Repair & Installation in Meridian, ID

Garbage disposal repair covers jammed grinding plates, tripped motors, and leaks at the connections under the sink. Installation means mounting a new unit, connecting it to the drain and dishwasher, and making sure it has the right electrical supply.

Some disposal problems you can fix yourself in two minutes. Others need a plumber. This page helps you tell which is which.

Safe fixes to try before calling

Never put your hand inside a disposal. These steps are safe with the power off.

  • If it is silent, press the red reset button on the bottom of the unit.
  • If it hums but does not spin, turn it off and use the hex wrench slot on the bottom to rock the plates free.
  • Use tongs, not fingers, to remove anything visible in the chamber.
  • Check the wall switch and the breaker if nothing happens after a reset.
  • Run cold water for 30 seconds before testing it again.

If the unit still hums, keeps tripping, or leaks, the motor or seals are likely failing and it is time for a service call.

A leak from the bottom of the housing cannot be repaired, because the internal seals have failed. That is the most common reason a disposal gets replaced.

What not to put down a disposal

Disposals handle small food scraps, not everything that fits. Grease, cooking oil, and fat are the worst offenders. They pass through as liquid, then cool and coat the drain line further down.

Fibrous food like celery, onion skins, and corn husks wraps around the plates. Rice, pasta, and potato peels swell into a paste. Coffee grounds and eggshells settle in the trap and build up.

Meridian's Public Works asks residents to keep fats, oils, and grease out of the sewer for the same reason. If the kitchen sink keeps backing up despite a working disposal, the problem is likely in the line, and our drain cleaning page explains how that is cleared.

Restaurant kitchens in downtown Eagle face the same grease problem at commercial scale.

A disposal that drains slowly even after it grinds normally usually points to a partial clog further down, which kitchen drain cleaning clears.

Installing a new disposal

Installation includes mounting the unit to the sink flange, connecting the drain and dishwasher line, and confirming the electrical connection. If your dishwasher drains through the disposal, the knockout plug inside the inlet has to be removed, a step that is easy to miss on a new unit.

Sizing matters. A small household that uses the disposal lightly does fine with a smaller motor. Larger households, or kitchens that cook from scratch every day, benefit from more horsepower and better grinding.

If your sink has no switch or outlet for a disposal, adding one is electrical work that may need an electrician.

Questions before you call

Garbage disposal questions

Why does my disposal hum but not spin?

Something is jamming the plates. Turn off the power and use the hex wrench slot underneath to free it. If it keeps jamming, the motor may be failing.

Can a disposal that leaks from the bottom be fixed?

No. A leak from the bottom means the internal seal failed, and the unit needs to be replaced.

Is it safe to put grease down the disposal with hot water?

No. Grease cools and sticks further down the drain line, which is how kitchen clogs form.

How long do garbage disposals last?

Many last around 10 years with normal use. Heavy use and fibrous food shorten that.

Can you install a disposal where there was none?

Yes, if there is space under the sink. An outlet or switch may need to be added by an electrician.

Why does my dishwasher drain into the disposal?

It is a common setup that uses the disposal's inlet as the dishwasher drain connection. It needs a clean hose and a working air gap or high loop.
