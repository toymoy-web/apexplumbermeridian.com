Sewer Line Replacement in Meridian, ID

Sewer line replacement means removing or abandoning the old building sewer and installing a new pipe from the house to the city connection. It is the right answer when the damage runs the length of the line, not just one spot.

It is a bigger job than a repair, so it should always start with camera footage that proves the whole line has failed. We show you that footage before recommending replacement.

How replacement differs from a sewer repair

A repair fixes one damaged section, such as a single cracked joint or a short run with roots. Replacement renews the whole lateral. The choice comes down to how much of the pipe is sound.

If the camera shows one problem in an otherwise healthy line, a spot sewer repair is usually the better value. When the footage shows breaks, offsets, and bellies scattered from the house to the street, patching one spot only moves the next failure a few feet down the line.

The same pattern holds east along Chinden, where older laterals in Garden City are frequent candidates for full replacement.

Repeat backups, slow drains, and sewage smells in older Ada County homes near Main Street are the usual signs that start a replacement conversation.

Signs a Meridian sewer line needs full replacement

These findings, usually from a camera inspection, point toward replacement rather than repair.

  • A collapsed section that a cable or camera cannot pass.
  • Several bellies where the pipe sags and holds water along its length.
  • Clay or cast iron pipe with cracks or heavy corrosion at many joints.
  • Roots entering at joint after joint, not just one.
  • Repeat backups even after spot repairs and jetting.

If you have not had the line filmed yet, start with a sewer camera inspection. It is the evidence every replacement quote should be built on.

Why older Meridian neighborhoods see more replacements

Most of Meridian was built after 1990, and the city notes that modern PVC laterals tend to last for many years with little maintenance. Full replacements in newer subdivisions are uncommon and usually follow a settled trench or a crushed section.

Old Town and other homes built before about 1970 are a different story. Clay and cast iron sewer pipe from that era has had decades to shift, corrode, and let roots in at every joint. When one section fails, the rest of the line is often close behind.

Mature trees add to it. The shade trees that make older blocks near Main Street pleasant also send roots looking for water, and an old clay joint is an easy target.

Replacement methods and trade-offs

The best method depends on depth, soil, what is above the pipe, and the pipe's condition.

MethodHow it worksTrade-offs
Open trenchExcavate the length of the line and lay new pipeMost versatile, most yard and hardscape disruption.
Pipe burstingPull a new pipe through the old one while splitting it apartTwo small pits, not suited to every soil or bend.
Cured-in-place liningInstall a resin liner that hardens into a new pipe inside the old oneLeast digging, needs a pipe that is still round and passable.
Partial replacement plus liningDig out the worst section, line the restUseful when one area is collapsed and the rest is sound.

Which method fits is confirmed after the camera inspection and a site visit.

New lines are installed with proper slope and a cleanout, following the Idaho State Plumbing Code, which builds on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). The finished line is filmed again so you have a record.

What replacement day looks like

Before digging, buried utilities are located through Idaho's 811 service. Inside Meridian city limits, the replacement is permitted work, and the permit and inspection run through Meridian Building Services.

The connection to the city system is coordinated with the city when work reaches the public right of way. Many replacements take one to two days, depending on method and length. Your drains are unusable while the old and new lines are being connected, so plan for a short period without sewer service.

After the inspection, the trench or pits are backfilled and the surface is restored as agreed in the quote.

What drives the cost of sewer replacement

Replacement is one of the larger plumbing investments. These are the biggest cost drivers.

  • Length and depth of the line.
  • Method: open trench, bursting, or lining.
  • Driveways, patios, or trees over the line.
  • Connection work at the city side.
  • Permit, inspection, and surface restoration.

Questions before you call

Sewer line replacement questions

How do I know my sewer line needs replacing, not repairing?

A camera inspection shows it. Damage along much of the line points to replacement. One isolated problem usually means a spot repair.

Can a sewer line be replaced without digging up my yard?

Often. Pipe bursting and lining need only small access pits. The camera and site visit determine whether your line qualifies.

How long does sewer replacement take?

Many jobs take one to two days. Longer or deeper lines, or hard surfaces over the pipe, can extend that.

Do I need a permit?

Yes. Inside Meridian city limits, sewer replacement is permitted and inspected through Meridian Building Services.

Who pays for the part under the street?

The City of Meridian maintains lines in the public right of way. The lateral on your property is the owner's responsibility.

Will my homeowners insurance cover it?

Many policies exclude wear, roots, and settling. Some offer service line coverage as an add-on. Check your policy.
