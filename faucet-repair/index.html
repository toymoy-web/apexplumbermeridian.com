Faucet & Sink Repair in Meridian, ID

Faucet and sink repair covers the fixture you touch every day. That means a faucet that drips, sprays sideways, or will not shut off, plus the drain and supply lines hiding in the cabinet below. Most of these are quick fixes. A few, like a slow leak under a sink, can quietly damage a cabinet floor for months.

We repair what is worth repairing and install new faucets when replacement makes more sense.

Why faucets wear out faster in Meridian

Meridian's well water carries 6.70 to 10.16 grains per gallon of hardness depending on your zone, according to the city's 2024 report. As water evaporates on and inside a faucet, it leaves calcium behind.

That scale builds up in aerators, which is why flow slowly weakens. It also collects inside cartridges, making handles stiff and wearing the seals that stop drips. A cartridge that might last many years in soft water can start leaking sooner here.

Soaking an aerator in white vinegar dissolves light scale. For a cartridge, replacement with the manufacturer's part usually solves the drip. A water softener slows the whole process, and our water softener page explains sizing by zone.

Homes on untreated private wells, common on the rural edges of Star, can see this cartridge wear even faster.

In Ada County's hard water, the same cartridge wear shows up whether the faucet sits in an Old Town kitchen or a new build off Eagle Road.

Cartridge drips are a common call from kitchens in Paramount, BridgeTower, and the newer subdivisions near Owyhee High School alike.

What to check under the sink

Many sink leaks start below the counter and go unnoticed until the cabinet floor swells.

  • Feel the braided supply lines for dampness, and look for rust on the ends.
  • Check the P-trap slip nuts for drips while the faucet runs.
  • Look for water stains or swelling on the cabinet floor.
  • Test both shutoff valves. They should turn fully and stop the water.
  • On kitchen sinks, check the dishwasher drain hose and disposal connections.

Stuck shutoff valves are common in older homes and worth replacing during any faucet work. They are the first line of defense if a supply line ever fails.

Repair or replace the faucet?

A quality faucet with a worn cartridge is usually worth repairing, especially if parts are still available. Replacement makes sense when the body is cracked, parts are discontinued, or the finish is corroded.

When you replace, bring the new faucet or choose one with the plumber. Installation includes new supply lines, checking the shutoff valves, and testing for leaks above and below the counter.

Choosing a replacement faucet

Match the number of holes in the sink or counter, or plan for a deck plate to cover extras. Ceramic disc cartridges generally hold up better in hard water than older washer designs.

Pick a brand with parts sold locally, so a future cartridge replacement is a quick repair instead of a special order.

What affects the cost of faucet and sink work

Faucet repairs are usually modest. These factors change the total.

  • Cartridge repair versus full faucet replacement.
  • Brand and part availability.

  • Damage to the cabinet floor from a slow leak.
  • Customer-supplied faucet versus one we provide.

Questions before you call

Faucet and sink questions

Why does my faucet keep dripping?

Usually a worn cartridge, washer, or O-ring. Meridian's hard water speeds up that wear.

Can you install a faucet I already bought?

Yes. Have it on site and we will install it with new supply lines.

Why is the water flow weak from one faucet?

The aerator is probably clogged with scale. Cleaning or replacing it usually restores full flow.

Is a small leak under the sink urgent?

Not an emergency, but do not ignore it. Slow leaks swell cabinet floors and can lead to mold.

Should I replace my shutoff valves?

If they are stiff, leak, or will not fully stop the water, yes. They matter most when a supply line fails.

Do you fix outdoor faucets too?

Yes, including frost-free hose bibs, which can split if a hose is left on in winter.
