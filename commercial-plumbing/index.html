Commercial Plumbing in Meridian, ID

Commercial plumbing covers the water and drain systems in businesses and rental properties: restaurant kitchens, office restrooms, retail spaces, medical offices, and multi-unit buildings. The problems are similar to a home's, but the stakes are different. A backed-up kitchen line can close a restaurant for a night.

Grease is the top commercial kitchen problem

Commercial kitchens send far more fat, oil, and grease down their drains than homes do. Even with a grease interceptor, lines downstream slowly coat over, and the clogs return on a predictable schedule.

Meridian's Public Works asks every customer to keep fats, oils, and grease out of the sewer system. For restaurants, that means interceptor maintenance plus periodic line cleaning. Hydro jetting is the standard method, because it scours the whole pipe wall rather than punching a hole through the grease.

A regular jetting schedule, set by how fast your line coats, is almost always cheaper than an emergency call on a Friday night.

Commercial water heaters and restroom fixtures

Businesses lean hard on hot water. A salon, dental office, or restaurant can run a tank heater through far more cycles in a day than a household does, and Meridian's hard water, 6.70 to 10.16 grains per gallon by zone in the city's 2024 report, shortens that equipment's life further.

Regular flushing and anode checks matter even more in commercial settings. When a heater fails, the same repair-or-replace logic on our water heater repair page applies, scaled up to the building's demand.

Restroom fixtures take the same beating. Flush valves, faucet cartridges, and floor drains in busy restrooms wear out faster, and a single running flush valve can quietly add to the water bill every hour the building is open.

Commercial backflow testing

Many commercial properties have backflow assemblies protecting the city water supply from equipment, irrigation, or fire lines. The City of Meridian requires approved assemblies to be tested annually by a certified tester, with results filed with the Water Division.

Failed tests carry the same 10 business day repair window as residential ones. Our backflow testing page explains the city's process in detail.

Commercial calls go smoother when access is planned. Let us know where cleanouts, water heaters, and shutoffs are, whether a key or escort is needed, and whether work must avoid customer areas during open hours.

For restaurants, early morning or after-close visits keep kitchen lines out of service only while the kitchen is quiet.
