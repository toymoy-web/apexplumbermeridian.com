The problems are similar to a home's, but the stakes are different. A backed-up kitchen line can close a restaurant for a night.

Commercial emergencies we answer around the clock

A business problem has a deadline that a home problem usually does not, such as opening time, a health inspection, or tenants upstairs. These are the commercial calls our 24/7 line dispatches right away.

Kitchen line backing up

Stop the dish machine and prep sinks. Grease clogs in restaurant lines can usually be opened the same night.

Restroom flooding

No hot water before opening

Food service needs hot water to stay open. Tell us the heater type and size on the call.

Water coming through a ceiling

In multi-unit buildings, the source is often the unit above. Have a key or contact ready for that space.

Grease is the top commercial kitchen problem

Commercial kitchens send far more fat, oil, and grease down their drains than homes do. Even with a grease interceptor, lines downstream slowly coat over, and the clogs return on a predictable schedule.

Meridian's Public Works asks every customer to keep fats, oils, and grease out of the sewer system. For restaurants, that means interceptor maintenance plus periodic line cleaning. Hydro jetting is the standard method, because it scours the whole pipe wall rather than punching a hole through the grease.

A regular jetting schedule, set by how fast your line coats, is almost always cheaper than an emergency call on a Friday night.

Leak detection for businesses and multi-unit buildings

Commercial leaks usually come to light through a utility notice or a bill that jumps with no change in business. In a retail strip, an office building, or a fourplex, the hard part is working out which unit or which line is responsible.

We start by isolating sections of the system with valves and the meter, one tenant space or building at a time, before any equipment comes out. Water surfacing in a parking lot or landscaped area points to a buried supply or irrigation line, which is traced and marked before anything is dug.

Fire sprinkler mains are maintained by fire protection contractors, so if the leak is on a fire line we will tell you so and point you to the right trade. For domestic water and drain lines, detection and the repair can usually happen on the same visit.

Commercial water heaters and restroom fixtures

Businesses lean hard on hot water. A salon, dental office, or restaurant can run a tank heater through far more cycles in a day than a household does, and Meridian's hard water, 6.97 to 10.53 grains per gallon by zone in the city's 2025 report, shortens that equipment's life further.

Regular flushing and anode checks matter even more in commercial settings. When a heater fails, the same repair-or-replace logic on our water heater repair page applies, scaled up to the building's demand.

Restroom fixtures take the same beating. Flush valves, faucet cartridges, and floor drains in busy restrooms wear out faster, and a single running flush valve can quietly add to the water bill every hour the building is open.

Commercial backflow testing

Many commercial properties have backflow assemblies protecting the city water supply from equipment, irrigation, or fire lines. The City of Meridian requires approved assemblies to be tested annually by a certified tester, with results filed with the Water Division.

Failed tests carry the same 10 business day repair window as residential ones. Our backflow testing page explains the city's process in detail.

Tenant improvements and new rough-ins

We also take calls for plumbing on tenant improvements, small additions, and new fixture rough-ins, such as a break room sink or an added restroom. That work is permitted and inspected, so it is scheduled around the inspection dates rather than squeezed into an emergency slot.

Bring the drawings or a sketch of where fixtures go when you call. The water heater size and the existing drain line usually decide what is practical.

Access, safety, and working around customers

Commercial calls go smoother when access is planned. Let us know where cleanouts, water heaters, and shutoffs are, whether a key or escort is needed, and whether work must avoid customer areas during open hours.

For restaurants, early morning or after-close visits keep kitchen lines out of service only while the kitchen is quiet.
