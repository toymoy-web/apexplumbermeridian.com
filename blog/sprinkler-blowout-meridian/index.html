Sprinkler Blowout in Meridian, ID: When to Do It and the Step Most People Skip

By the Meridian Apex Plumbing team · Published September 24, 2026.

The City of Meridian recommends blowing out sprinklers and winterizing the backflow assembly before October 31. That date is a safety margin, not a magic line. The goal is to have every zone and the backflow device empty of water before the first hard freeze, when temperatures drop well below 32 degrees overnight.

Most homeowners remember the blowout. Fewer remember the backflow assembly itself, and that is the part that cracks. This guide explains both and the order to do them in.

Why a missed blowout gets expensive

Sprinkler pipe is shallow. Lateral lines to the heads often sit only a few inches below the lawn, and the mainline and valves are not much deeper. When water freezes in them it expands, and brittle fittings and valve bodies split.

The damage stays hidden all winter. It shows up in April, when the first spring start-up sends water spraying out of a cracked valve box or a split backflow assembly. By then the repair competes with every other homeowner who made the same mistake.

The backflow assembly is the most exposed part. It sits above ground on purpose, since that is how pressure vacuum breakers and reduced pressure assemblies are designed to work. Above ground also means it gets the coldest air first.

Blowing out early costs you a few weeks of watering at most. Blowing out late can cost a backflow assembly.

How a blowout works

A blowout pushes compressed air through each zone until only mist comes out of the heads. If you do it yourself, go slowly and never run air through the backflow assembly.

  • Shut off the water supply to the sprinkler system at its isolation valve.
  • Connect the compressor to the blowout port downstream of the backflow assembly, not through it.
  • Run one zone at a time at a moderate pressure, and stop when the heads only mist.
  • Do not let air run through a dry zone for long, since friction can heat and damage fittings.
  • Repeat each zone a second time to catch water that settled back into low spots.

If you are not comfortable with a compressor, hire it out. A blowout done at too high a pressure can do as much damage as a freeze.

The step people skip: winterizing the backflow assembly

After the zones are blown out, water can still sit inside the backflow assembly. This is what cracks in winter.

  • Close the isolation valve upstream of the assembly if you have not already.
  • Open the small test cocks with a flat screwdriver so trapped water drains.
  • Turn the assembly's own shutoff valves to a half-open, 45 degree position, which lets water drain from the ball valves.
  • Leave the test cocks partly open through winter.
  • Cover the assembly with an insulated pouch or bag, leaving drain points unobstructed.

The first spring start-up is the reverse. Close the test cocks, return the shutoffs to open, and bring water back slowly so the assembly is not slammed with pressure.

If the assembly cracked anyway

A split brass body is the classic sign of a freeze. So is water spraying from the bonnet of a pressure vacuum breaker the first time you turn the system on. Shut the isolation valve and leave it off.

A cracked body cannot be repaired. Replacing it requires a permit from Meridian Building Services, and if the system runs on city water, the new assembly will need its annual test too. Our backflow repair and replacement service handles the permit, the install, and the test in one visit when possible.

Check the rest of the system while you are at it. A freeze that cracked the assembly may also have split a valve or a mainline fitting. If you notice a soggy spot in the lawn after start-up, or the water meter keeps moving with the sprinklers off, our leak detection page explains how to find the break.

Spring start-up, done in the right order

Most freeze damage is discovered at start-up, so the way you bring water back matters. Wait until overnight lows stay above freezing, usually sometime in April.

  • Close the backflow assembly's test cocks and set its shutoffs back to fully open.
  • Open the main sprinkler isolation valve slowly, a quarter turn at a time.
  • Watch and listen at the assembly for a minute before running any zone.
  • Run each zone briefly and walk it, looking for geysers, pooling, or heads that do not pop up.
  • Check the water meter with the system idle to confirm nothing is running underground.
  • Schedule the annual backflow test now if your sprinklers run on city water.

Opening the supply quickly slams the assembly and the lateral lines with pressure, and it can split a fitting that survived the winter. Slow start-ups prevent a lot of spring repairs on their own.

Newer homes are not exempt

Homeowners in brand-new subdivisions sometimes assume a new system is protected. It is not. The pipe and assemblies are the same, and a first-year homeowner who does not know the routine is the most likely to miss it.

That pattern shows up across the valley, including in fast-growing Star, where many homes are going through their first few winters. The fix is simple: put the blowout and backflow winterizing on the calendar for mid-October every year.

Questions before you call

Sprinkler blowout questions

When should I blow out my sprinklers in Meridian?

Before the first hard freeze. The City of Meridian recommends finishing before October 31.

Can I blow out my sprinklers myself?

Yes, with a suitable compressor and care. Use a port downstream of the backflow assembly and keep the pressure moderate.

Do I need to winterize the backflow assembly separately?

Yes. Water can remain inside the assembly after a blowout. Open the test cocks, set the shutoffs to half open, and insulate it.

What if we get an early freeze before I blow out?

Shut off the sprinkler supply and drain what you can, then complete the blowout as soon as possible.

Is a cracked backflow preventer covered by insurance?

Usually not, since freeze damage from not winterizing is typically treated as maintenance. Check your policy.
