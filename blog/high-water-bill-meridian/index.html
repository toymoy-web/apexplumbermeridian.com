High Water Bill in Meridian? A Step-by-Step Way to Find the Leak

By the Meridian Apex Plumbing team · Published September 24, 2026.

When a Meridian water bill jumps without a change in how your household uses water, the cause is usually a running toilet, a sprinkler zone that does not fully shut off, or a leak somewhere in the pipes. The fastest way to narrow it down is the meter test: turn off all water, watch the meter's leak indicator, then close the main shutoff at the house and watch again.

This guide walks through that test and the other free checks, in the order that finds the most common causes first.

Step 1: rule out the obvious

Before hunting for a leak, make sure the higher bill is not explained by use.

  • Compare the same month last year, not just the previous month.
  • Check whether summer sprinkler use on city water explains the jump.
  • Consider guests, a new family member, or a filled pool or hot tub.
  • Confirm the bill is based on an actual reading, not an estimate.

If none of those explain it, something is using water you do not see.

Step 2: the meter test

Your water meter is usually in a covered box near the street. Turn off every faucet, the dishwasher, the washer, the softener if it is regenerating, and the sprinkler system. Then open the meter box and watch the small leak indicator, often a triangle or star-shaped dial.

If the indicator moves, water is flowing somewhere. Next, close the main shutoff at the house and look again. If it stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the house, in the buried service line.

A leak on the street side of the meter, or at the meter itself, belongs to the city. Call Meridian Water at 208-888-5242 for that. Everything from the meter to the house is the owner's responsibility.

Step 3: if the leak is inside, check these

These are the most common indoor causes, roughly in order of how often they turn up.

Typical indoor causes of a high bill.

Toilets are the usual suspect. A worn flapper lets water leak into the bowl so the tank keeps refilling, and Meridian's hard water speeds up that wear. Our toilet repair service fixes most of these in one short visit.

Step 4: do not forget the sprinklers

If your sprinklers run on city water, they can be the biggest source of a surprise bill. A zone valve with debris in it may not close completely, leaving one zone trickling all night. A cracked lateral line after a freeze can leak underground without any visible spray.

Look for a patch of lawn that stays soggy or unusually green, heads that seep water after the cycle ends, or a valve box with standing water. Run the meter test with the sprinkler system on standby and again with its supply valve closed to see whether it is the source.

Step 5: when to call for leak detection

If the meter test shows a leak but the toilets, fixtures, and sprinklers all check out, the leak is hidden. It may be in a crawl space, inside a wall, under a slab, or in the yard. That is when professional equipment earns its cost, because it finds the spot without opening walls or digging trenches to search.

Our leak detection service uses pressure testing, acoustic listening, and thermal imaging to pinpoint the leak before anything is opened. Homes on larger lots, common on the edges of Kuna and south Meridian, often have longer service lines where a slow leak can hide for weeks.

Two less obvious water users

A water softener that gets stuck in regeneration can send water to the drain for hours. You may hear water running near the softener in the garage when nothing else is on. Checking the control head or unplugging and restarting it often ends the cycle, and a service call fixes a failing valve.

Sprinkler controllers are the other quiet culprit. A controller that reset to a default program after a power outage may be watering every day instead of every few days. Check the schedule after any outage, and look at the rain sensor if you have one.

Keep an eye on use going forward

Once the leak is fixed, a simple habit catches the next one early. Read the meter at the same time on two evenings a week apart and note the difference. You will quickly learn what normal looks like for your household in each season.

A sudden jump between readings, with no guests or extra watering to explain it, is the earliest warning you will get. It usually shows up long before the next bill arrives.

After the leak is fixed

A few follow-ups help you confirm the problem is solved.

  • Repeat the meter test a day after the repair.
  • Watch the next bill for a return to normal.
  • Keep receipts in case your utility offers a leak adjustment.
  • Check toilet flappers yearly, since hard water keeps wearing them.

Questions before you call

High water bill questions

How much water does a running toilet waste?

It depends on how badly the flapper leaks, but because it runs day and night, even a slow leak shows up on the bill. A fast leak can be the single largest water user in the house.

Why is my Meridian water bill so high all of a sudden?

A running toilet, a sprinkler zone that will not shut off, or a hidden leak are the most common causes. The meter test narrows it down.

How do I check my water meter for a leak?

Turn off all water, open the meter box, and watch the leak indicator. If it moves, water is flowing somewhere.

Is a leak between the meter and my house my responsibility?

Yes. The City of Meridian maintains the public side. The line from the meter to your house belongs to the owner.

Can a toilet really raise my bill that much?

Yes. A leaking flapper can let water run constantly, day and night, until it is fixed.

Does the city adjust bills for leaks?

Utilities sometimes offer adjustments. Keep repair receipts and ask Meridian's utility billing office.
