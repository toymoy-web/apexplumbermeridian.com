Winter in Star: the first freeze in a new house

Star has one of the youngest housing stocks in the Treasure Valley. The town had 1,795 residents in 2000, 5,793 in 2010, and 11,117 by 2020, and it only incorporated in December 1997. Its mayor has recalled dirt roads there in the mid-1990s.

That means many Star homeowners are going through their home's first few winters. The weak points show up the first time temperatures drop hard: a crawl space vent that was never closed, a hose left on a frost-free spigot, a line to an upstairs bathroom running over a cold garage.

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock across Star, on both the Ada and Canyon County sides, by way of Chinden and Star Road from Meridian.

Most of those calls are preventable. Our frozen pipe repair page has the fall checklist, and if a line has already split, burst pipe repair is available 24/7.

On acreage lots, a pressure tank and well pump check often comes first, while subdivision homes more often need a camera look at the drain line or a water heater flush.

Before We Arrive: If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak in Star

Many Star homes are in their first few winters, and owners are still learning where things are. These steps are worth reading now, before you need them.

Shut off the main valve

In subdivision homes it is usually in the crawl space or a closet. On acreage with a well, switch off the well pump breaker to stop the flow.

Kill power to wet areas

Turn off breakers for rooms with water near outlets, and set a gas water heater to pilot if the main will stay off.

Clear the immediate area

Move belongings away from the leak and open cabinet doors so you can see where the water is coming from.

Call our 24/7 line

Call (208) 679-8357 and mention whether you are on public water or a private well, so the plumber arrives prepared.

Spring: sprinklers, backflow, and cracked assemblies

The first sprinkler start-up of the year is when frozen backflow assemblies reveal themselves. A split brass body sprays the moment water is turned back on.

If your Star home's sprinkler system connects to potable water, it needs a backflow preventer that is tested once a year. Replacing a cracked one, or one that fails its test, is covered on our backflow testing page. Winterizing it each fall prevents most spring surprises.

Fall: getting a young house ready for winter

The best time to prevent a Star winter call is October. Blow out the sprinkler lines, winterize the backflow, disconnect hoses, and look at the crawl space before it gets cold.

Builder-grade water heaters installed during Star's 2010s growth are now approaching their first replacement. If yours is rumbling or slow to recover, fall is a better time to replace it than during a January cold snap. Our water heater replacement service includes a free estimate for planned swaps.

Star plumbing questions

Is emergency service available in Star?

Yes. Frozen lines, burst pipes, and failed water heaters in Star are answered 24/7, including the first cold nights of the season when calls spike.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Star?

Star uses a single ZIP, 83669, and we cover all of it: the subdivisions on the Ada County side toward Eagle, the in-town streets near State Street, and the acreage lots that stretch across the Canyon County line.

Why did my new Star home's pipes freeze?

Usually an open crawl space vent, a hose left on a spigot, or a line over a cold garage. New homes are not immune, especially in their first winters.

Do you work on homes with wells and septic?

Yes, on the plumbing inside the home. Well pumps and septic tanks are handled by those specialists, and we will tell you when that is the issue.

When should I winterize sprinklers in Star?

Before the first hard freeze. Many cities in the area recommend finishing by the end of October.

Is my builder-grade water heater due for replacement?

If it is 10 or more years old and rumbling or slow to recover, it is worth an inspection. Planned replacements come with a free estimate.

Can you help with low water pressure on an acreage property?

Yes. We check the house plumbing and pressure settings, and refer pump problems to a well specialist.
