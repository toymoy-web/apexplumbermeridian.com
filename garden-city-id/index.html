Garden City's housing is older, and the plumbing shows it

Garden City grew decades before most of the Treasure Valley. It had 764 residents in 1950 and 4,571 by 1980, then reached 12,316 in 2020. Because Boise and Eagle surround it, the city has had nowhere to expand, so much of its housing is older stock mixed with infill.

Before We Arrive: If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak in Garden City

Older Garden City homes often have gate-style shutoff valves that have not been turned in years. Know where yours is and try it before an emergency, not during one.

Shut off the main valve

Turn it gently. If an old gate valve will not move, use the curb stop in the meter box rather than forcing it.

Kill power to wet areas

Switch off breakers for wet rooms. In manufactured homes, check that water is not reaching wiring under the floor.

Clear the immediate area

Move belongings and rugs away from the leak, and keep people out of any water that came from a floor drain.

Older sewer laterals near the river

Many Garden City homes still send waste to the street through original clay or cast iron laterals. Mature trees on older lots find those joints, and root intrusion is one of the most common reasons a Garden City drain keeps clogging.

Before anyone quotes a big repair, a camera inspection shows what is actually happening in the line. If the camera finds a break or heavy roots, sewer line repair can often fix one section rather than the whole run.

Garden City is its own city inside Ada County, so check your water bill to see whether the City of Garden City or Veolia serves your street before calling about a problem at the meter.

From Chinese gardens to the Greenbelt

Chinden Boulevard takes its name from the Chinese gardens and farms that gave Garden City its name around the turn of the 20th century. The land along the river was irrigated early, and the area kept a mix of small lots, older homes, and mobile home parks for decades.

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock throughout Garden City, a straight run east from Meridian along Chinden Boulevard.

Today the city is changing fast along the river. New townhomes and apartments sit near the Greenbelt, the whitewater park, and Expo Idaho, home of the Western Idaho Fair each August. That mix of 60-year-old houses and brand-new construction on the same streets means the right fix depends heavily on which kind of building you are in, which is why we ask the age of the home first.

Garden City has an older population, and that changes priorities

At the 2020 census, Garden City's median age was 47 and more than a quarter of residents were 65 or older. For many households, the most valuable plumbing work is not an emergency repair but a safety upgrade.

Anti-scald shower valves, easy-to-reach shutoff valves, and a water heater set to a safe temperature reduce the risk of burns and floods. Our shower and tub installation page explains the pressure-balancing valves the plumbing code requires on new showers.

Garden City plumbing questions

Is emergency service available in Garden City?

Yes, at any hour. Garden City calls for failed old supply lines, sewer backups, and leaking water heaters are answered 24/7.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Garden City?

Yes. Most of Garden City mails under 83714, including the river neighborhoods and the Chinden corridor, while the eastern blocks where Garden City meets Boise use 83703. We cover both.

My Garden City house has low water pressure everywhere. Why?

In homes from the 1950s to 1970s, galvanized pipe that has narrowed with rust is the most common cause. A pressure test and a look at the pipe confirm it.

Why does my drain keep clogging in an older Garden City home?

Roots entering an old clay or cast iron sewer lateral are a frequent cause. A camera inspection shows exactly where.

Do you work on manufactured homes?

Yes. Lines under manufactured homes are often exposed and can freeze, and fittings there are a common leak point.

Can you make a bathroom safer for an older family member?

Yes. Anti-scald shower valves, accessible shutoffs, and a safe water heater setting are common upgrades.

Do you service the new townhomes near the Greenbelt?
