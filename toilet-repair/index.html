Toilet Repair & Installation in Meridian, ID

Toilet repair covers everything from a flapper that lets the tank run all night to a failed wax ring leaking water under the floor. Toilet installation means setting a new toilet on a sound flange with a fresh seal and supply line.

Most toilet problems are small and quick to fix. A few, like a leak at the base, can quietly damage the subfloor, so they are worth handling promptly.

What your toilet is telling you

Most toilet complaints trace back to a small number of parts.

Typical causes. The plumber confirms on site.

If the toilet gurgles when other fixtures drain, the problem is usually downstream in the main line. Our drain cleaning page explains that situation.

A running toilet is a water bill problem

A toilet that runs between flushes sends clean water straight down the drain, day and night. It is one of the most common reasons a Meridian water bill rises for no obvious reason.

Try the dye test. Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank and wait 15 minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking.

Meridian's hard water speeds up this wear. Mineral deposits roughen the flapper seat and stiffen rubber parts, so flappers and fill valves often need replacing sooner than in soft water areas. If you suspect a leak somewhere else, our leak detection page walks through the meter test.

The same hard water wear shows up in Star, where homes on private wells can have untreated water that is even harder on toilet parts.

Toilets in older Ada County homes near Main Street and in newer builds alike wear faster on the city's hard well water.

Running toilets are a frequent problem in busy family homes in neighborhoods such as Paramount and BridgeTower, where a single bathroom can see heavy daily use.

Leaks at the base need prompt attention

Water seeping out around the base of a toilet usually means the wax ring has failed. Every flush then pushes a little water under the toilet, into the subfloor.

Over time that water rots the plywood around the flange. The first signs are a soft spot in the floor, loose tiles, or a musty smell. In homes over crawl spaces, the damage may show from below as a stained joist.

The fix is to pull the toilet, inspect the flange, repair or replace it if needed, and reset the toilet on a new seal. Catching it early keeps a small repair from becoming a floor replacement.

When replacement makes more sense

A new toilet is worth considering in these cases.

  • The porcelain is cracked in the tank or bowl.
  • It clogs often despite a clear drain line.
  • Parts are no longer available for the model.
  • You want a comfort-height or elongated bowl.
  • An older high-volume toilet is driving up water use.

Installation includes removing the old toilet, checking the flange and floor, setting the new toilet on a fresh seal, connecting a new supply line, and testing for leaks. The old toilet is hauled away.

What changes the price of toilet work

Most toilet repairs are modest. These factors raise or lower the cost.

  • A simple part swap versus pulling and resetting the toilet.
  • Flange repair or subfloor damage found under the toilet.
  • The toilet model you choose for a replacement.
  • Shutoff valve replacement if the old one will not close.
  • After-hours service for an overflowing toilet.

Toilet repair questions

How do I stop an overflowing toilet?

Turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise. If it will not close, lift the tank lid and raise the float, then close the main shutoff.

Why does my toilet run by itself?

Usually a worn flapper lets water leak into the bowl, so the tank refills. Hard water wear makes this common in Meridian.

Is a toilet leaking at the base serious?

Yes. It usually means a failed wax ring, and water can rot the subfloor over time. Have it reset soon.

Can you install a toilet I bought myself?

Yes. We can install a customer-supplied toilet. Have it on site and we will check the flange and floor before setting it.

How long does a toilet replacement take?

Most take a couple of hours, longer if the flange or subfloor needs repair.

Do you handle toilets in rental properties?

Yes. We work with landlords and property managers across Meridian.
